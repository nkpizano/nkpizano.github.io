Be a Better Statistical Practitioner

Oct 24th, 2025 posted by N.K.Pizano

Data Ethics

In an age where data drives decisions, the role of ethics in statistical practice has never been more important. This post curates a collection of useful resources, guides, and frameworks to help students and professionals remain ethical when collecting, analyzing, and reporting data. From professional codes of conduct (like those from the American Statistical Association and Royal Statistical Society) to practical readings on bias, transparency, reproducibility, and data privacy, these materials are designed to help you think critically about the choices you make as a data practitioner. Whether you’re teaching statistics, conducting research, or working with real-world data, these links will help you navigate the complex intersection of ethics, integrity, and statistical rigor.
  • AMSTAT Ethical Guidelines for Statistical Practice The ASA developed a set of eight guidelines for ethical and professional statistical practice, regardless of job title, discipline, or training experience. Working these principles will help foster accountability, transparency, reproducibility, and other thoughtful, data-driven decisions.
  • Paper: AI, data governance and privacy by OECD This report explores how recent advances in generative AI have intensified data governance and privacy challenges. It highlights the current disconnect between AI policy and privacy policy communities, noting that their fragmented, jurisdiction-specific approaches hinder effective regulation and collaboration. To bridge these divides, the report aligns the OECD Privacy Guidelines with the OECD AI Principles, identifying shared values and potential synergies.
  • A Guide for Ethical Data Science The collaboration between the Royal Statistical Society and Institute and Faculty of Actuaries led to the development of a document that identified five broad themes and principles that were described in other data ethic frameworks. This document provides practical application of ethical practices related to societal value, avoiding harm, maintaining professional competence, trustworthiness, and accountability.
